## 1. Executive Summary

Farmers around the world navigate shifting temperatures, changing rainfall patterns, water shortages, extreme weather, and declining soil health. These pressures make it difficult to choose crop rotations that protect fields, conserve water, and support long-term resilience.

**The Challenge:** Create a decision-support tool that uses NASA Earth observations along with local soil information, crop characteristics, and farmer priorities to help farmers explore rotation strategies that could strengthen soil health and adapt their farms to changing conditions.

---

## 2. Background & Context

Farmers face increasingly complex decisions as temperatures shift, rainfall becomes less predictable, and soil health declines across many agricultural regions worldwide. Key compounding stressors include:
* **Water Shortages & Drought Stress:** Depleting surface reserves and groundwater aquifers restrict irrigation quotas.
* **Soil Degradation:** Topsoil erosion, organic matter depletion, compaction, and salinization reduce natural fertility.
* **Unpredictable Weather Extremes:** Heatwaves, unseasonal frost events, and severe precipitation anomalies challenge traditional planting calendars.

### The Crop Selection Dilemma
Selecting new crops to introduce into a rotation cycle is challenging because agricultural systems are deeply localized:
* A cover or cash crop that builds soil nitrogen and structure in one climate zone may consume excessive moisture in an arid region.
* Introducing a novel crop might create disease or pest bridges for subsequent cash crops.
* A candidate crop might conflict with local equipment capabilities or lack accessible processing facilities and commercial markets.
* Crops may fall outside shifting frost-free windows or required growing degree days (GDD).

### Multigenerational Inspiration
This challenge is inspired in part by multigenerational farming experience—exemplified by Nevada farmer Denise Moyle navigating high-desert alfalfa fields. Across generations, farming families have endured seasons where access to longitudinal environmental tracking and predictive climate intelligence could have averted crop loss and identified resilient, regenerative rotation sequences.

Today, NASA’s multi-decade Earth observation archives offer unprecedented fidelity into local environmental parameters, and modern statistical and computational techniques provide the means to translate raw satellite telemetry into actionable, farm-level insights.

## 3. Core Objectives

Participants are tasked with building an app, predictive model, interactive spatial map, or integrated decision-support tool that combines NASA Earth observations with ground-level agricultural parameters.

### Key Goals:
1. **Explore Adaptive Crop Rotations:** Help producers evaluate how diversifying or reordering crop sequences can restore soil biology, reduce erosion, conserve irrigation water, and withstand climatic anomalies.
2. **Multi-Strategy Comparative Analysis:** Model and contrast at least three distinct rotation pathways (including the baseline/historical sequence).
3. **Seasonal Vulnerability Identification:** Surface high-risk windows when fields sit fallow and exposed, or when established crops face acute thermal or moisture thresholds.
4. **Actionable & Grounded Recommendations:** Ground every recommendation in verifiable satellite metrics while clearly communicating data uncertainties, informational gaps, and local agronomic risks.

---

## 4. Fundamental Guiding Principle: "Support, Never Replace"

The intended outcome is strictly a **decision-support tool**—one that clarifies trends, uncovers latent opportunities, and calculates trade-offs, but **never replaces farmer judgment or local extension expertise**. 

The system must present transparent, explainable recommendations that invite human verification and account for individual farm constraints (equipment, labor, capital, water rights, and market access).
